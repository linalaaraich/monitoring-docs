1The 60-second pitch

CIRES Technologies (Tanger Med, Digital Factory Observability Service) runs a small Java/Spring-Boot stack — 6 microservices behind Kong, on k3s, with a monitoring VM running Grafana / Prometheus / Loki / Jaeger. The alert volume is small (~10–40 fires per day in steady state) but every one of them hits the same small on-call team. Without an AI layer, every alert demands the same human investigation regardless of whether it's a real incident or a flake.

The triage service consumes Grafana alert webhooks and a separate stream of Drain3-detected log anomalies, then for each one it: gathers metric / log / trace context through a wall of MCP servers, picks the closest of 14 RCA archetypes, asks a local LLM (qwen2.5:7b-instruct on a GTX 1060) to produce a cause-first root-cause analysis, runs the answer through three validators, and emits a verdict — ESCALATE (email the on-call) or DISMISS (record the reason and move on). Operators can override either way; their feedback flows back into the suppression layer.

End-to-end latency under real load (2026-05-19 heavy-load investigation, 95 production RCAs): median 6–7 min, worst 23 min; cheap-path short-circuits (dedup, recurrence gate, suppression replay) complete in milliseconds. Sprint 4's P0 anchor bundle targets median 90 s after per-stage instrumentation + serial Ollama concurrency + 180 s hard timeout — see §7.5. The triage container has been running steady-state on adolin-wsl since Sprint 3 kick-off on 2026-04-23 (~27 days at time of writing).

2What we built (and what we deliberately didn't)

What we built: a single FastAPI service (monitoring-triage-service) that ingests webhooks, talks to five MCP servers (one each for Prometheus, Loki, Jaeger, the RCA history DB, and a deployment-events stub) over JSON-RPC, calls a local LLM via Ollama, persists decisions to SQLite, exposes a self-hosted dashboard, and emits 27 Prometheus metrics for its own observability. Around 9,500 lines of Python plus ~1,800 lines of declarative config (exemplar library, hallucination blocklist, alert-template fallback, recurrence-gate annotations).

What we deliberately didn't build:

  • An agentic loop with multi-step planning. We make at most one retry call (bounded agency) before committing to a verdict. The cost ceiling is predictable and the system stays reproducible at temperature=0.
  • A vector-DB / RAG layer. The exemplar library is a curated YAML of 14 archetypes injected at prompt time; we explicitly chose a frozen seed over learned retrieval until we have enough operator feedback to curate well.
  • A microservice mesh of small AI services. One pipeline, one process, deterministic ordering. Easier to reason about; easier to validate.
  • Tool-calling. The LLM produces structured JSON via prompt-engineered output schemas, not native tool calls. We'll move to native tool-calling when we move off the laptop to a larger model.
Design bias to flag for review

The system prefers determinism + auditability over raw model capability. Every quality mechanism (validators, blocklist, clamp, exemplars) exists to make the LLM's mistakes recoverable. If you'd weight that trade-off differently, we want to know.

5How we keep it honest

Five reinforcing mechanisms catch different failure modes. Each one exists because a real RCA went wrong in a specific way; the mechanism was the fix.

5.1 Exemplar library — structural scaffolding

Frozen seed of 14 RCA archetypes in app/exemplars/library.yaml. Each archetype carries a model RCA paragraph (3–5 sentences), an evidence pattern, a suggested-actions pattern, a confidence band, and an alertname-regex matcher. The pipeline picks the best-matching archetype before the LLM call and renders it as a ## Reference exemplar section in the prompt. The LLM sees "this is what a good RCA for this kind of alert looks like, structurally" before it sees any evidence. The factual claims in the exemplar are not the answer — they're the shape.

The 14 archetypes (see §6) were curated by hand from real incident playbooks + Sprint 2's "happy-path scenarios." Three were added during the 2026-04-28 audit cycle after coverage gaps surfaced.

5.2 Hallucination blocklist — alert-keyed phrase rejection

YAML at app/hallucination_blocklist.yaml. Per-alertname banned phrase list. Examples:

  • HighP95Latency + HighKongP95Latency reject /actuator/* in the RCA — actuator endpoints are scrape-internal, citing them is always a tell that the LLM is reading the wrong evidence.
  • Drain3AnomalyDetected rejects PromQL, Prometheus query, Drain3Alert, Drain3Threshold — log-anomaly RCAs that lead with metric prose are leading with the wrong evidence pillar.

A hit triggers a retry with an alert-specific HINT in the feedback string. The LLM doesn't get to ignore the violation; it has to address it explicitly.

5.3 Surface-only LEDE + hypothesis-menu validators

The surface-only LEDE regex catches RCAs that restate the alert without attributing cause. Real example we caught and fixed in F-2: "Based on observed metrics, the HighP95Latency alert indicates that service kong is experiencing elevated p95 latency." That's surface-only — it restates the alert and signals nothing about why. Six widening rounds cover PromQL-leading prose, "based on observed/repeated/context/...", "the alert for X", "looking at the metrics", and the alertname-first form.

The hypothesis-menu validator (shipped 2026-04-29 as S3-HF-03) catches the opposite failure mode: the LLM hedging by listing multiple possible causes without committing. Seven regex patterns target "possibly X or Y", "may be due to slow query, pool exhaustion, or GC pressure", "either A or B" prose with 5+ word noun phrases on either side. Negative lookahead excludes idiomatic "either way" / "either case". Pair this with a cause-evidence rule: the named cause's tokens must overlap with the evidence tokens (tokenized as [a-z]{4,} so hikaricp_connections_active splits into [hikaricp, connections, active] for cross-matching).

5.4 Confidence clamp + diagnostic steps fallback

The clamp is the user-visible trust signal. When any validator hits, or RCA quality lands as data_starved, or suggested actions look templated, the clamp forces confidence ≤ 0.40 and replaces suggested_actions with a small list of diagnostic_steps — read-only verbs like "Check Grafana panel X", "Tail Loki for <service> in the last 10 min". The email + dashboard render diagnostic steps as a separate card with a "Withheld — confidence clamped to 0.40" notice in the actions section. The operator sees clearly that the system is not confident and not recommending any change-of-state.

5.5 Bounded-agency retry — exactly one extra call

When the first pass is flagged data_starved or INCONCLUSIVE, instead of just scolding the model in the retry prompt, we let it request one additional MCP call from a fixed 6-tool whitelist (Prom instant query, Loki range, Jaeger get-traces, RCA-history lookup, deploy-events, get-trace). The model returns a structured tool request; we execute it; we re-prompt with the new evidence. Properties: exactly-one-extra-call ceiling, Pydantic-validated args (no arbitrary command execution), still reproducible at temperature=0.

7Case study — the 0b215ef3 incident

Real RCA · 2026-04-29 · the failure that drove Epic 4

The alert: HighKongP95Latency — p95 at the Kong gateway crossed threshold. Upstream service was spring-boot.

What the LLM said: Cause-first, fluently — but it recommended kubectl rollout restart deployment/spring-boot and kubectl describe pod -l app=spring-boot | grep -i oom in the suggested actions. These are OOMKill remediations on a p95 latency alert. The actions were templated from the wrong archetype — the OOM template (app/suggested_actions.yaml:64-77) only matches ^(High|Medium|Critical)MemoryUsage$ regex; it didn't fire here. The LLM produced these directly.

Why the system shipped it anyway: The F-4 confidence clamp correctly fired (RCA was tagged data_starved because the pipeline only gathered service-boundary trace summaries — it never called get_trace(trace_id) to drill into spring-boot's spans). The clamp lowered confidence to 0.40, which is the trust signal. But it didn't strip the bad actions — the operator saw "low-confidence verdict" next to "here are some kubectl commands to run." Bad combination.

What we shipped to fix it:

  • S3-HF-01 (Tier 0): the clamp now strips suggested_actions entirely and emits a separate diagnostic_steps field with read-only verbs.
  • S3-HF-03 (Tier 2): hypothesis-menu validator + cause-evidence rule (the LLM here also hedged with "possibly upstream is slow due to GC or pool exhaustion").
  • S3-HF-02: defensive tests pinning the action-template lookup so a YAML regex widening can never key wrong-archetype actions.

Epic 4 close-out — shipped 2026-05-19 (Sprint 3, 15/15 SP):

  • S3-HF-07 (Tier 1, 3 SP): get_trace(trace_id) MCP tool plus PII sanitizer (db.statement literals → ?, http.target IDs → :id) plus a new ## Trace span breakdown prompt section. The LLM now sees per-span attributes on the failing trace and can say "hikaricp connection pool exhausted: 27/30 active, all spans queued" rather than "upstream is slow." 16 new tests.
  • S3-HF-04 / S3-HF-05 / S3-HF-06 (4 SP combined): the last three MCP-only invariant bypasses closed (entity_baselines.py → prometheus-mcp; bounded-agency rca_history.similar path → rca_history_mcp; get_similar_decisions(min_quality) + get_low_rated_examples_for_alert on RCA-history MCP). Six in-process exemptions (config, drain3 poller, boot-time) explicitly documented.
  • S3-HF-08 (2 SP): MCP-integrity CI lint at scripts/check_mcp_invariant.py + GitHub Actions workflow + 4 self-tests with synthetic-violation probes — regression guard for the firewall.
  • S3-HF-09 (3 SP): chaos-corpus regression gate as the 5th evaluation axis. The hallucination firewall now has its own behavioural test corpus.

See §7b for a live RCA from 2026-05-20 showing this work in production.

7bCase study — Epic 4 in production (2026-05-20)

Real RCA · 2026-05-20 · the deliberate inverse of 0b215ef3

The alert: PodHighMemoryUsage (critical) on spring-boot, instance 10.0.1.194:10250. Container working set sat at 99.63% of cgroup limit — the same shape of alert that drove 0b215ef3, with the same tempting "just restart the pod" remediation hanging in front of the LLM. RCA id 8891233d, fired 2026-05-20T12:47:04Z.

What the LLM produced: Verdict ESCALATE, cause-first prose pointing at frequent /actuator/health, /actuator/prometheus and /api/employee requests, and high span counts on Kong. But the prose hedged — "could be a sign of either frequent monitoring or potential performance bottlenecks", "might be a significant number of concurrent requests", "could lead to increased resource utilization". That is textbook hypothesis-menu language.

What the system actually emitted: the hypothesis-menu validator (S3-HF-03) flagged the prose; the F-4 confidence clamp then fired, forcing llm_confidence to exactly 0.40 and stripping suggested_actions to []. In their place, four read-only diagnostic_steps rendered. The fourth is the load-bearing one, verbatim:

  • "Do NOT run kubectl rollout/scale/set or restart spring-boot until a specific cause (slow query / GC pause / pool exhaustion / downstream timeout) is named with trace or metric evidence."

That sentence is the lesson from 0b215ef3 baked into the diagnostic_steps fallback template. The operator sees a clamped verdict next to read-only investigation verbs and an explicit guard-rail — not a kubectl command. That is the entire point of Epic 4.

Correlation surfaced as context, not cause: three nearby TargetDown fires on the monitoring service landed in correlated_alerts (timestamps within 3 min). The pipeline passed them to the operator as evidence of a possible cluster without letting the correlation become a causal claim in the RCA prose. Hypothesis-menu discipline working on a live fire.

Secondary success — a different mode, same epic: earlier that morning at 08:50 UTC the audit-live cron fired a synthetic MediumCpuUsage (RCA 275ddbaf). Verdict DISMISS at confidence 0.92 — not clamped, the system was genuinely confident. The prose cited "+0.4σ above the same-hour baseline from one week ago" and "matches the prior 32 firings in the last 7 days, all of which were dismissed." Suggested actions pointed at the alert rule itself (stddev_over_time + offset 7d > 3), not at the workload — the P7 "dismiss-with-recommendation" pattern. This is the adaptive-threshold-noop archetype plus closed-loop feedback: the 32 prior dismisses arrive via the suppression layer and inform the next verdict.

What worked:

  • S3-HF-01 (Tier 0), in production: clamp stripped suggested_actions on a hedged memory-pressure RCA — no kubectl in the operator-facing output.
  • Diagnostic-steps fallback template: the "do NOT run rollout/scale/set" guard-rail prose, lifted from the 0b215ef3 postmortem, rendered verbatim on a live critical fire.
  • S3-HF-03 hypothesis-menu validator: caught the "could be either X or Y" hedging that 0b215ef3 also exhibited; the clamp fired automatically.
  • Closed-loop dismiss (P7 archetype): MediumCpuUsage dismissed at 0.92 with a rule-fix recommendation, informed by 32 prior suppressed firings.

Where it's still slow / still drifting:

  • P0 latency (Sprint 4 anchor): investigation duration on the primary RCA was 1,557 s (~26 min). The verdict and the output shape are right; the speed is not — too late to be load-bearing in an active incident. See §7.5 P0.
  • P1 prose-quality drift: the RCA cited routine /actuator/* and /api/employee log volume as if causal to 99.63% memory pressure. The clamp blocked the bad remediation, but the LLM still produced surface-level evidence selection. Sprint 4's P1 (PromQL/numerical-noise scrubber + context sanitizer) tightens this.

Known output-quality issues + Sprint-4 fixes

Drafted 2026-05-19 from a real-load investigation: 95 production RCAs across 2026-04-28 → 2026-05-19, plus a heavy real-load test that produced two real TargetDown RCAs. Twelve findings, ranked P0–P11 below. Full plan: Sprint 4 candidates table.

Honest framing for an SRE reviewer

The system is structurally sound — verdicts are right more often than wrong, the safety mechanisms fire as designed. But the output prose is not yet trustworthy for an unsupervised dev workflow. Three deep problems compound: (1) latency (median 6 min, worst 23 min), (2) PromQL/numerical noise leaking into prose, (3) suppression cascade over-eagerly silencing real follow-up fires. These are the Sprint 4 anchors.

8What we need from you

Four concrete inputs. We've tried to scope each so it doesn't cost you a week.

  • 1. Review the 14 archetypes. Do they cover the kinds of incidents you'd expect to see in a Kong + Spring-Boot + k3s + monitoring-VM stack? What's missing? In particular: anything around stateful storage failures, distributed coordination (etcd, scheduler), certificate / DNS / network, or cascading dependencies we should be modelling?
  • 2. Walk us through your RCA procedure for 3–5 archetypes. Pick the ones you have the strongest mental model for (e.g. OOM loop, p95 latency spike, log anomaly post-deploy, disk filling cascade). Tell us: what evidence you'd want, in what order; what hypotheses you'd rule out first; what makes you commit to a cause vs. keep digging. 10Glossary & acronyms

RCARoot-cause analysis. The LLM-produced prose paragraph + verdict + evidence + suggested actions.
MCPModel Context Protocol. JSON-RPC server pattern from Anthropic. We have five — one per data pillar — and they're the only sanctioned data path between the LLM and the world.
Exemplar / archetypeOne of 14 curated "good RCA shapes" injected into every LLM prompt.
Confidence clampMechanism that forces confidence ≤ 0.40 and strips actions when quality mechanisms catch a problem.
Bounded agencyThe exactly-one-extra-MCP-call retry path. Not a loop; not an agent in the multi-step sense.
Drain3Log-template clustering library (LogPAI / IBM). We run it server-side over Loki to detect novel log templates as a fourth signal alongside metrics / logs / traces.
Recurrence gatePer-rule "suppress fires 1–4, force-escalate fire 5" mechanism for opted-in flappy alerts. Pre-LLM (cheap) and post-LLM (safety net) variants.
F-N / S3-HF-NNInternal story IDs. F-1 through F-5 are the 2026-04-28 RCA-quality hardening pass. S3-HF-NN are Sprint 3 hallucination-firewall stories.
Surface-onlyAn RCA that restates the alert symptoms without naming a cause. The validator catches these.
Hypothesis menuAn RCA that hedges with multiple possible causes. The validator catches these (shipped 2026-04-29, S3-HF-03).
data_starvedA quality classification meaning the LLM didn't have enough evidence to commit to a cause. Triggers the clamp and the bounded-agency retry.
