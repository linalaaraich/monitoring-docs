What we learned (the honest read)

  • Cause depth tracks the evidence. When the failure reason is in kube-state-metrics or logs (cart OOMKilled; spring-boot Hibernate error + recent rollout), the LLM named the precise root. When the reason lives only in pod events (ad ImagePullBackOff, currency Pending), it named the outage and deferred the exact reason to a diagnostic step instead of guessing. That is the anti-hallucination firewall working, and a clean improvement lever: a bounded-agency “describe pod events” call would close the gap. Zero hallucinated causes across the batch.
  • RollingUpdate protects availability. A bad image or bad nodeSelector on a 1-replica Deployment does not cause an outage on its own — Kubernetes keeps the old healthy pod until the new one is Ready. To get a real outage (and the ESCALATE path) the broken template has to take over (Recreate strategy / delete the old pod). Worth saying at the jury: a no-op deploy that never goes Ready is correctly a non-incident.
  • Novelty, not volume. productCatalogFailure produced no alert because Drain3 has already learned that error template (0 new patterns) and the error-rate stayed under the metric threshold. This is the designed trade-off, and a real gap to name honestly: a repeated, known, low-rate failure is silent. Tomorrow’s Drain3 batch uses genuinely novel templates (the malformed-payload flood) to exercise the positive path.
  • Serial investigation backlog. A burst of co-firing alerts queues behind a single GPU (~30–41 s per investigation), so decisions for later services arrived a few minutes after the breach. Honest and expected on one host; prod scales the model/host. One cart alert re-fired and paged a second time during slow post-revert recovery — the only redundant page of the batch.
