AI RCA · Service Reference

The Triage Service — how it thinks about your alerts

A FastAPI service that turns a raw Grafana webhook into a structured root-cause verdict in seconds. Built on Ollama + five MCP servers + Drain3 anomaly detection, with fingerprint-keyed dedup, two-layer suppression, an exemplar-injected prompt, a four-scan response validator, a bounded-agency retry path, action-template fallback, and a timeout-safe escape hatch. This page documents the 10-stage pipeline, HTTP surface, data model, dashboard, and the metrics it exposes — everything you need to navigate /decisions, the dashboard, and the logs with confidence.

1. Overview

The triage service is the brain of the AI RCA pipeline. Grafana (or Drain3's anomaly webhook) POSTs an alert at it; the service spends at most PIPELINE_TIMEOUT seconds gathering correlated context from Prometheus, Loki, and Jaeger, picking the best canonical RCA archetype from an exemplar library, asking an LLM to reason over the evidence, validating the response, and storing the verdict. Downstream, one of five things happens:

  • Escalate — verdict "something's genuinely wrong" → email the on-call engineer an HTML report with RCA, remediation-only suggested actions, and evidence.
  • Dismiss — verdict "expected / noise" → record the decision, send no email, the pager stays quiet.
  • Suppress (Layer 1 fingerprint) — same Grafana fingerprint re-fired inside the dedup window; we persist a short-path suppressed_duplicate row linked to the prior decision and skip the LLM.
  • Suppress (Layer 2 history) — different fingerprint but same (alertname, service) recently dismissed; we replay that verdict without paying the GPU cost.
  • Timeout passthrough — the asyncio guard fired before the LLM returned → we forward the raw Grafana alert as a safety fallback. Alerting never gets blocked by the AI layer.

Every decision — escalate, dismiss, suppress, or timeout — is persisted in a local SQLite RCA history. That history doubles as (a) the dashboard at /dashboard, (b) the Layer 2 suppression signal, (c) the "how often does this alert fire" context for future LLM prompts, and (d) the storage shape that the planned closed-loop feedback (US-5.3) writes into.

Why a separate service?

The triage service is intentionally a thin coordinator — it doesn't own Prometheus, Loki, or Jaeger. Each pillar is queried through a small MCP server (prometheus-mcp, loki-mcp, jaeger-mcp), so swapping storage backends, adding auth, or rate-limiting a pillar doesn't touch the pipeline code. See the architecture diagram for the full picture.

2. The 10-stage pipeline

Each Grafana alert the service accepts runs through these stages. Stages 1–3 are cheap and synchronous (webhook receipt + dedup + Layer 2 history check); stages 4–10 run under asyncio.wait_for(..., timeout=PIPELINE_TIMEOUT) with a raw-alert fallback path if the budget elapses. End-to-end budget defaults to 2400 s; the single-LLM-call sub-budget is 1800 s. Real-load measurement (Phase 2 GPU-migration smoke test, 2026-05-21, on g5.xlarge/A10G + qwen2.5:14b): ~38 s end-to-end. Prior 7b/laptop GTX 1060 numbers (median 6–7 min, worst 23 min, 95 production RCAs 2026-05-19) drove the structural decision to migrate; see D24.

1
Webhook receive 200 µs
POST /webhook/grafana or /webhook/drain3 returns HTTP 202 immediately and enqueues the pipeline as a FastAPI background task. triage_webhooks_received_total is incremented here.
2
Deduplication Layer 1 · fingerprint
DedupManager.check(fingerprint, status) keys on Grafana's stable label-hash fingerprint (not alertname + instance) with a TTL of DEDUP_WINDOW_SECONDS (default 600 s / 10 min). Returns (is_duplicate, first_decision_id). On a duplicate, the pipeline persists a short-path suppressed_duplicate row linked to first_decision_id and exits — Grafana keeps re-firing while an alert is active; we only pay GPU once per window. After a fresh fingerprint produces an RCA, record_first_decision() writes the link back so subsequent dups know which prior decision to point at. triage_alerts_deduplicated_total increments per drop.
3
Pre-LLM suppression Layer 2
Looks up the same (alertname, service) in the RCA history over the last TRIAGE_HISTORY_LOOKBACK_MINUTES. If the prior verdict was dismiss or triage_suppressed, we replay that verdict without calling Ollama. Saves the GPU from burning cycles on known-noise alerts. Gated by TRIAGE_SUPPRESSION_ENABLED.
4
Context fan-out parallel · 3 MCPs
context_window.gather(alert) fires three httpx.AsyncClient calls in parallel — to prometheus-mcp :8091, loki-mcp :8092, and jaeger-mcp :8093 — anchored on the alert's startsAt, not on wall-clock "now". All three pillars share an absolute time window of [startsAt − 5 min, startsAt + 2 min] (clamped to ≤ now). MCP timestamps come back in three different units (Prometheus float-seconds, Loki nanoseconds, Jaeger microseconds); the gatherer normalises them. Typical per-MCP latency is 150–280 ms. The correlation is why metrics, logs, and traces all describe the same incident, not three different ones.
5
Drain3 log annotation
Raw Loki log lines are handed to DrainAnalyzer.analyze(). Drain3 clusters each line against a learned template set, returning {cluster_id, template, is_new_pattern, match_count}. New clusters or rare templates (match_count < drain3_anomaly_threshold, default 5) are tagged [ANOMALY] and surfaced as anomaly_summary. The LLM then sees pre-digested log signals instead of a raw dump. Drain3 also self-fires when the per-window anomaly rate exceeds drain3_alert_rate_threshold (default 0.10) over drain3_alert_min_lines (default 100); that hits /webhook/drain3 as a synthetic Drain3AnomalyDetected alert and re-enters the pipeline at stage 1.
6
Historical frequency + Drain3 recurrence lookup
RCAStore.get_alert_frequency() counts how many times this alertname has fired in the past 7 days and grabs its last-seen timestamp. The LLM prompt gets "this alert has fired N times in the last 7 days — last seen {ts}" so its verdict can be flaky-test-aware. Drain3-sourced alerts additionally pull a 7-day recurrence count for the matched template — three or more recurrences within 7 days flips the prompt's framing toward escalation per D8.
7
Exemplar injection D17 · prompt-eng
exemplars.find_for_alert(alertname, service, deployment_type, signal, severity) scores all 14 archetypes in app/exemplars/library.yaml using a weighted match (alertname regex 0.10 + services 0.40 + deployment_type 0.30 + signal 0.20 + severity 0.10) and returns the highest. The matched exemplar is rendered as a ## Reference exemplar section before the ## Pre-Gathered Context in the prompt, giving the LLM a structural target tailored to this alert before it sees the evidence. The 14 archetypes (11 original + 3 added during the 2026-04-28 audit cycle to close coverage gaps): oom-loop, upstream-latency-attribution, service-self-p95-saturation (new), critical-resource-saturation (new), otel-collector-degradation (new — partially forward-looking), synthetic-blip-dismiss, cascade-incident-disk-loki, drain3-novelty-post-deploy, bounded-agency-resolves-inconclusive, adaptive-threshold-noop, closed-loop-feedback-override, crashloop-bad-config, network-firewall-attribution, tls-cert-expiry-pre-failure. Default fallback is generic-sre-shape. See D17; full prose at monitoring-triage-service/docs/happy-path-scenarios.md.
8
LLM investigation qwen2.5:14b · temp=0
LLMClient.investigate() assembles the SYSTEM_PROMPT (philosophy + rules A–J, including D19's cause-first rule and remediation-only rule) plus the rendered exemplar plus pre-gathered context, POSTs to Ollama's /api/generate with structured-output mode and a stable prefix prompt cache, and parses into LLMDecision (decision, severity, confidence, rca, reason, anomaly_summary, suggested_actions, evidence). Sloppy outputs (dict-in-list, missing fields, unit drift on timestamps) are coerced by LLMDecision's pydantic validators rather than rejected — small quantized models are forgiving and we'd rather take their signal than 500 on the user.
9
Validate & salvage 5 scans · retry · fallback
response_validator.validate(decision, alert) mutates the LLM output through five scans:
  • banned-phrases — known hedges that hide a real verdict ("metrics suggest", "potentially indicates"…) get logged and counted.
  • vague-action — action entries that read like prose rather than an executable command get pruned.
  • investigation-only — read-only "actions" (kubectl get, docker logs, Query Grafana: …, ssh … 'top') get pruned per the D18 remediation-only philosophy. Validated against 32/32 representative pairs.
  • arch-mismatch — actions whose syntax doesn't match the affected service's service_deployment_type get pruned (no docker restart spring-boot when spring-boot is a k3s pod).
  • surface-only-lede (D19) — first-sentence regex catches RCAs that lead with raw PromQL or restate the alert; triggers a one-shot retry with a stricter cause-first prompt.
If after validator the verdict is data_starved AND triage_bounded_agency_enabled=true, the pipeline does one bounded-agency retry: the LLM is given access to a 6-tool whitelist (prometheus.range_query, loki.query, jaeger.search, rca_history.similar, rca_history.list_exemplars, rca_history.get_exemplar) and asked to issue exactly one MCP call before re-deciding. triage_bounded_agency_invocations_total counts these retries.

If suggested_actions is still empty after validator + retry, the action-template fallback at app/suggested_actions.yaml fills it from a remediation-only YAML keyed on alertname × deployment_type. triage_fallback_total counts these. The pipeline never escalates with an empty action list.
10
Egress + persist
If decision == ESCALATE, build a polished HTML email (app/notifier.py) — including correlated alerts in the same incident window — and send via SMTP. A notifier failure (SMTP hiccup, template bug) is logged and swallowed so the next step still runs. RCAStore.save_decision(record) then writes the full RCARecord to the SQLite rca_history table. Finally, if the alert had a fingerprint, DedupManager.record_first_decision(fingerprint, record.id) writes the dedup back-link so subsequent dups inside the window persist short-path rows pointing at this RCA. That row is what powers /decisions, the dashboard, the Layer 2 suppression check, and the closed-loop feedback storage shape (US-5.3).
Timeout safety

Stages 4–10 run under asyncio.wait_for(..., timeout=PIPELINE_TIMEOUT). If it fires, we (a) send the raw Grafana alert email as a fallback, (b) record a timeout_passthrough row, and (c) continue serving. There's also a circuit_breaker in front of the LLM call (app/llm_client.py): three consecutive Ollama failures open the circuit for OLLAMA_CIRCUIT_OPEN_SECONDS seconds, during which the pipeline short-circuits to the raw-alert path. The AI layer is never in the critical path for paging.

5. Data model

Three pydantic models you'll see in logs and the codebase:

RCARecord — what gets persisted

One row per pipeline run. Defined in app/models.py. Every field is pre-flattened (no nested objects), which is what makes the SQLite schema straightforward. See the table in §4 for field meanings — the /decisions response is a list of these.

LLMDecision — what Ollama returns

The LLM is asked for a structured JSON object matching this shape. It's produced at stage 8, mutated by the validator at stage 9, and merged into RCARecord at stage 10.

FieldTypeMeaning
decisionenumESCALATE / DISMISS / INCONCLUSIVE
severitystrRe-assessed severity — critical / warning / info
confidencefloat [0,1]Optional; many small quantized models omit it
rcastrNatural-language root-cause narrative
reasonstrOne-liner: why this verdict
anomaly_summarystrSummary of Drain3-tagged anomalies seen in logs
suggested_actionslist[str]Concrete next steps, rendered in the escalation email
evidencelist[str]Bullet points: metrics values, trace IDs, specific log lines
Pragmatic tolerance

Small 7B-quantized models occasionally return evidence as [{"metric": "cpu", "value": 87}] instead of ["cpu=87"]. A pydantic field_validator coerces the dict shape to a compact string rather than 500-ing the pipeline. If the model wants to give us structured evidence, we take it — we just don't make downstream code deal with the shape variance.

GatheredContext — what the pillars returned

Internal only; not persisted. Holds metrics/logs/traces as they came back from the three MCPs plus the per-pillar latency and any errors. Feeds the prompt builder in app/llm_client.py.

rca_quality — post-hoc RCA quality tag

Every persisted decision gets classified actionable or data_starved post-hoc (after the LLM responds) by a regex on the RCA text. data_starved means the model hedged with phrases like "insufficient data", "no recent metrics", or "cannot determine" rather than naming a cause. The classifier is defensive — false negatives (missing the tag on a hedge) are much cheaper than false positives that'd teach the LLM to avoid the right phrases.

The tag is used three ways:

  • Visible on the dashboard as a small ⚠ DATA STARVED pill next to the verdict, so you can scan a week of decisions and spot the hedges instantly.
  • Fed back into the LLM prompt on the next invocation for the same (alertname, service) pair, quoted verbatim with an explicit "don't repeat these phrasings" instruction. This is the only way to teach a stateless LLM from its own past mistakes.
  • Triggers a one-shot retry loop: if the first pass is tagged data_starved, the pipeline runs the LLM once more with an escalated anti-hedge prompt appended before persisting the decision. The retry is gated on triage_data_starved_retry_enabled (default on) and only runs if the pipeline has enough budget left.
Why this matters

The 2026-04-23 run produced two MediumCpuUsage decisions that both said "insufficient data in metrics, logs, and traces" — not because the alert was wrong, but because the webhook carried service="unknown" and every MCP query returned empty. After the quality sweep (alert-rule enrichment + triage fallbacks + retry loop), the same webhook now produces an RCA citing the observed metric value from the alert payload, with fallback queries when service-scoped MCP lookups miss.

6. The dashboard

GET /dashboard renders a zero-dependency HTML page that pulls the latest 100 decisions, shows running stats at the top, and lets you click any row to expand the full RCA + reasoning inline. No JavaScript framework, no build step — just FastAPI returning HTML.

What's in it

  • Four stat cards: total decisions, count escalated, count dismissed, count timed out.
  • Filter bar: type to narrow the table by alert name, service, or verdict in real time.
  • Decision table: timestamp, alert, source, service, severity, verdict, action taken, duration.
  • Click any row → an inline panel opens beneath it showing: full rca_report, full llm_reasoning, triage decision, alert fingerprint, LLM confidence, decision ID.
Where to open it

From any tailnet peer: http://observability-gpu-uswest2:8090/dashboard (MagicDNS, preferred) or http://100.65.77.50:8090/dashboard (raw tailnet IP). On the instance itself: http://localhost:8090/dashboard via Tailscale SSH (ssh ubuntu@observability-gpu-uswest2) or SSM Session Manager as break-glass. The operator manual lives at /dashboard/guide — a sage-themed quick reference for every column, code, and abbreviation. The us-west-2 g5.xlarge (A10G) is the only host that runs the dashboard since the 2026-05-21 migration (D24); adolin-wsl is rollback only.

8. Dedup & suppression — the two layers

Alerts are noisy. Grafana re-fires them while they're active; the same root cause can trip five correlated rules at once. The triage service has two complementary ways to avoid burning GPU on redundant work.

Layer 1 — in-memory dedup (app/dedup.py)

A DedupManager keyed on Grafana's stable fingerprint (a hash over the alert's labels) plus its current status. Window is DEDUP_WINDOW_SECONDS (default 600 s / 10 min). While an alert is actively firing, Grafana re-POSTs it every group wait; we skip all but the first within the window. The earlier (alertname, instance, status) shape was retired during the P1.6 work — fingerprint is more reliable because it survives label tweaks and avoids collisions when two unrelated alerts share an alertname. The manager returns (is_duplicate, first_decision_id) so a duplicate persists a short-path suppressed_duplicate row pointing at the original RCA. Cheap, deterministic, process-local.

Layer 2 — pre-LLM suppression (pipeline._check_suppression)

Queries rca_history for the same (alertname, service) in the last TRIAGE_HISTORY_LOOKBACK_MINUTES (default 30 m). If we recently produced a dismiss verdict, or suppressed it for the same reason, we replay that decision without calling Ollama. This is the mechanism that keeps a flaky-test noise pattern from consuming GPU cycles repeatedly. The planned closed-loop feedback (US-5.3) extends this with a similarity check that lets operator thumbs-down verdicts re-weight future suppression decisions; the planned recurrence gate (US-5.8) adds a per-rule "suppress fires 1–4, then force-escalate fire 5" hysteresis for opted-in flappy alerts.

Both layers are optional via env (TRIAGE_SUPPRESSION_ENABLED=false, DEDUP_WINDOW_SECONDS=0). Useful when debugging — you want every alert to run end-to-end.

9. A real verdict — the 2026-04-23 laptop smoke test

Field note · 2026-04-23

The first end-to-end run of the GPU-migration pipeline was on a laptop (MSI with GTX 1060 / 6 GB VRAM) using qwen2.5:7b-instruct. The smoke-test script test-alert.sh fires a synthetic LaptopSmoke_* alert with a fabricated BackendHigh5xxRate payload, waits for a verdict, prints it.

The verdict the LLM produced — visible in the JSON example above — was dismiss, with the reasoning "This is a manual synthetic test, expected behavior.". The RCA continued: "The alert is part of a manual integration smoke test designed to validate the end-to-end AI-stack pipeline on a laptop GPU. The presence of logs indicating 'unavailable' and empty metrics/traces is expected during this test as it bypasses normal service interactions."

What makes this impressive: we never told the model this was a synthetic test. The alert payload itself looked like a real production alert (real alertname shape, real service name, real Grafana webhook structure). The model inferred the synthetic nature from the absence of correlated evidence — Prometheus returned empty metrics for the service, Loki had no matching log lines, Jaeger had no traces. Rather than hallucinate a root cause, it correctly concluded "this is probably a test" and dismissed the alert. That's exactly the behaviour you want from an AI triage layer: if there's no evidence, don't invent one.

The pipeline completed in 67 995 ms (≈68 s). The historical CPU-only baseline on k3s with llama3.2:3b was 20–40 minutes per alert; on the 1060 with qwen2.5:7b, we were down to roughly a minute. The originally-scoped cloud A10 upgrade (qwen2.5:14b-instruct) was projected to halve it again. That migration was first dropped 2026-05-19 on cost grounds, then reversed and shipped 2026-05-21 after a Lambda-autoshutoff cost-frame rebuild — see D24. Phase 2 smoke on the new A10G runner pulled end-to-end median to ~38 s (~8× speedup vs the 7b/laptop baseline shown here). The 7b laptop runner is retained as warm fallback.

What to take away from this

  • The LLM doesn't need to be told what it's looking at — structured evidence plus "tell me what's going on" is enough.
  • Empty context is a signal, not a failure. The service already ships sources_available and per-pillar errors in GatheredContext; the LLM uses them.
  • The dashboard is the fastest way to eyeball this. Click the row, read the reasoning — don't squint at /decisions JSON.
  • The Layer 2 suppression path matters. This exact alertname re-fires in Sprint 3's scheduled rehearsals — the second run should skip Ollama entirely and replay the dismiss verdict in milliseconds.
