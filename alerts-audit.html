Executive summary

Compared to the 2026-04-24 audit (the previous end-to-end review, on 50 decisions across roughly 5 days of operation), this audit covers 5.4× the decision volume over 28 days and finds the system in a structurally healthier shape — but with one specific shape of failure that's now load-bearing and clearly identified.

What's changed since 2026-04-24:

  1. Sprint 3 Epic 4 (hallucination firewall) shipped 15/15 SP on 2026-05-19. All three known MCP-only-invariant bypasses closed; bounded-agency retry now routes through MCP, entity baselines now route through MCP, and a CI lint at scripts/check_mcp_invariant.py guards against regression. Lint status today: clean. See the hallucination firewall page for the six-mechanism landing.
  2. Drain3 now self-fires. April's deep finding was "Drain3 has NEVER fired a self-alert despite processing 123 k lines." Threshold lowered to 20 lines + 2 %, and 8 underlying anomalies have since produced 13 Drain3AnomalyDetected webhook fires (12 escalates, 4 dismisses on the LLM path). The "0 self-alerts in 123 k lines" finding is closed.
  3. The Spring-Boot OOM-kill loop is fixed. April fix (2 GiB container limit + -XX:MaxRAMPercentage=75) held: spring-boot pod restart count is now 16, up from 45 in 47 hours in April. The current pod has been continuously up for 45 hours.
  4. Suppression-cascade fix (P2) shipped 2026-05-19 and validated 2026-05-20. Synthetic audit-live-* fingerprints are now filtered out of the suppression layer, the lookback window narrowed from 30 → 10 min, and the recurrence-gate annotation widened to TargetDown, HighP95Latency, HighKongP95Latency. Acceptance gate closed at 2026-05-20 09:00 UTC (6/6 post-fix audit-live decisions landed investigate, zero triage_suppressed).
  5. Suppression layer is doing the bulk of the work. 114 of 271 decisions (42 %) were caught by the layered suppression — 39 recurrence-gated pre-LLM, 57 post-LLM, 18 dedup — without an Ollama call. That's an effective LLM-call-rate reduction of 42 % vs. naïve "every webhook gets an inference".

What's gotten worse / what is now load-bearing:

  1. Pipeline latency is the dominant operational risk. Median LLM-path RCA = 3.4 min, p75 = 9 min, p95 = 22.3 min, max = 26 min (n=142). 14 decisions hit the hard timeout and were emailed-raw to the operator. Under three concurrent fires (today's 08:42–08:57 UTC window on 2026-05-20, repeated at 12:00 UTC), the /decisions endpoint went unresponsive while Ollama processed a queued MediumCpuUsage. The 180 s timeout shipped during this audit's window, but it caps the worst case rather than fixing it; the root cause is single-LLM serialisation on a laptop GTX 1060.
  2. The MCP-only invariant still bounds the LLM, but the prose layer above it leaks raw telemetry. Recent RCAs cite Z-score equations verbatim ("(6.72-6.3)/0.65 = +0.6σ"), unconverted floats (0.0015848932803859128), PromQL function calls, and # TYPE/# HELP markers. This is an architectural shape issue in the prompt-assembly layer — Sprint 4 P1 (~4 h) targets it.
  3. Diagnostic-step generation is in the wrong layer. Two different TargetDown fires (111ded21 + a4555bdd) produced word-for-word identical 4-step diagnostic blocks. The LLM is templating, not synthesising. Sprint 4 P3 (~6 h) moves generation into a deterministic helper.
  4. Today's audit-live cron did not run. The triage service was offline at the 2026-05-21 12:00 UTC scheduled fire; no 2026-05-21-live.md exists. The cron itself is fine — the system was down during its window. Two cheap-path decisions did make it in at 12:14 UTC (post-restart, both recurrence_gated_pre_llm), and the system is back up at audit-write time.

Why spring-boot still has 0 Drain3 anomalies on 93 k lines

Per top_new_patterns_per_service in /drain3/stats, spring-boot's "novel" templates are dominated by JDBC Hikari pool timeouts, JPA CannotCreateTransactionException, and routine dispatcher debug lines — patterns that have been seen continuously since the heavy-load investigation began on 2026-05-19, so they've already been learned by the Drain3 model and are no longer "novel".

The frontend and proxy services have lower volume and more bursty log shapes, so anomalies surface faster (frontend 2 anomalies / 12 k lines, proxy 6 anomalies / 7 k lines). This is the expected steady-state shape of a Drain3 baseline: novelty drops sharply as the model warms, and the only fires after warm-up come from genuinely new error shapes (a new exception class, a new deploy introducing new log lines).

Reframe — not a defect

April's "0 in 123 k lines" was a real defect (thresholds unreachable). May's "0 on spring-boot despite 93 k lines" is the correct behaviour of a warmed Drain3 model — the log shapes are all known. The right signal here would come from per-deploy baseline diff (Sprint 4 candidate #4 / P-4 in the predictive roadmap) — snapshot Drain3 templates per service before each deploy, compare after, flag novel templates as deploy-introduced anomalies.

Bounded agency + trace depth — what Epic 4 changed

April's audit had a separate "Traces aren't in drain3" section sketching what trace-based anomaly detection would take. That section is now obsolete in two directions:

  1. Trace depth landed via S3-HF-07 (2026-05-19). The Tier-1 deep-trace gather (get_trace via Jaeger MCP) now fires automatically on latency-family alerts (*P95Latency / *ErrorRate). The decision row carries the trace_id + slow-span breakdown into the LLM prompt instead of the LLM having to suggest "open Jaeger and inspect" as a diagnostic step.
  2. Bounded-agency retry now routes through MCP (S3-HF-05). When the validator catches an unsupported claim, the retry path no longer makes direct DB / Prometheus calls — it routes back through the same MCP wall. Every retry is auditable + reproducible, and the MCP-invariant CI lint (S3-HF-08) verifies this stays true in code.

Operator-perceived design issue surfaced 2026-05-20: a PodHighMemoryUsage RCA on spring-boot emitted "Open Jaeger and inspect the slowest trace…" as a diagnostic step instead of fetching the trace itself. Root cause: context.py:_LATENCY_ALERTNAME_PATTERN only fires get_trace for *P95Latency / *ErrorRate alerts — memory and CPU alerts get summary traces only, then the F-4 confidence clamp falls back to deterministic clamp_actions.py:diagnostic_steps_for_clamp() which suggests the operator do what the system could do. Deferred to Sprint 4 as part of the P0-bundle / bounded-agency rewrite (pair with #10 MCP native tool-calling). The deeper fix is to make bounded-agency fire on low-confidence verdicts (not just data_starved) so the system iterates on get_trace / prometheus.query / loki.query_range instead of emitting hint text.

Coda — what this audit proves
