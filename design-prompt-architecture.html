Paste-ready brief

You are designing TWO architecture diagrams for a Final Year Project (FYP) report at a Moroccan engineering school. The project is the CIRES Observability Platform — an AI-assisted root-cause-analysis layer running in production at CIRES Technologies (Tanger Med). The diagrams will be printed in the report, shown to an academic jury, and used in the supervisor defence. They must be visually striking, technically accurate, and printable at A4 portrait.

I will attach reference screenshots of the current diagrams (system-architecture.png, hallucination-firewall.png, triage-pipeline.png) in the next message so you can see the components + flow that need to be preserved — and the cramped layout we're moving away from.

================================================================
SECTION 1 — CONTEXT & GOAL
================================================================

The diagrams are for the FYP final report. Audience: academic jury + supervisor (a working SRE/DevOps lead). They must:

  - Fit cleanly on A4 portrait (210 × 297 mm / 8.27 × 11.69 in) — one page per diagram, full bleed acceptable but a 12 mm margin preferred for printing
  - Be printable AND visually striking on screen
  - Read at first glance — the jury will spend 30 seconds on each figure during the defence; they need the architecture story to land in that time
  - Be split into TWO diagrams for clarity (the original combined diagram tried to show everything at once and is too cramped to read at A4)

The two diagrams:
  DIAGRAM 1 — Phase 1: Observability foundation (the metrics/logs/traces stack)
  DIAGRAM 2 — Phase 2: AI triage layer (the FYP contribution that sits ON TOP of Phase 1)

================================================================
SECTION 2 — BRAND & VISUAL STYLE
================================================================

HYBRID style — light/off-white background (so they print cleanly on white paper) with the dashboard's accent colours for component categories. This matches the visual language already established on linalaaraich.github.io/monitoring-docs/ — specifically the sprint4-status.html and solution-brief.html surfaces.

Colour palette (use these exact hex values for component categories):
  #4ea8de  blue       — sources, ingress, edges into the system
  #6bcf7f  green      — MCP layer, healthy/success states
  #40d0d0  cyan       — terminal/storage states, terminal outputs
  #f0a050  orange     — pipeline stages, transformations
  #b07ee8  purple     — AI / LLM components
  #e06070  red        — safety boundary, hallucination firewall, clamps

Background: white (#ffffff) or near-white (#fafafa) for the page; subgraph clusters may have a very pale tinted fill (e.g. #fff5f5 for the MCP firewall cluster, matching hallucination-firewall.dot).

Typography:
  - Sans-serif throughout (Helvetica, Inter, or similar)
  - Component labels: 11-13 pt, bold
  - Subgraph cluster labels: 12-14 pt, semibold
  - Annotations / captions: 9-10 pt, regular, grey (#3a3f52)
  - Generous whitespace between layers — the diagram should breathe; no edge crossings if avoidable
  - Clear visual hierarchy: layer titles > component labels > annotations

Component shape language:
  - Rounded rectangles for components (8 px corner radius)
  - 1.5 px borders, 100% colour
  - Fills at full saturation for primary components; pastel tinted fills (~15% opacity) for "planned" / "future" components, with dashed borders
  - Arrows: solid 1.2 px for primary data flow; dashed for "planned" or "cross-phase" edges
  - Subgraph clusters: rounded dashed outline, 12-14 px margin, label top-left

REFERENCE: match the visual language of sprint4-status.html and solution-brief.html on linalaaraich.github.io/monitoring-docs/ — same pill style, same accent palette, same generous-whitespace feel.

SECTION 3 — DIAGRAM 1: PHASE 1 — OBSERVABILITY FOUNDATION
================================================================

Title at top: "Phase 1 — Observability Foundation"
Subtitle (smaller, grey): "The metrics, logs, and traces stack that feeds the AI triage layer"

LAYOUT: top-to-bottom (TB) flow, A4 portrait. Five horizontal layers:

  LAYER 1 (top) — APPLICATION (blue cluster)
    - react-springboot-mysql application on k3s (single rounded box, labelled "react-springboot-mysql on k3s")
    - kong gateway (single rounded box, labelled "Kong API Gateway")
    Note (10 pt grey, below the cluster): "Instrumented enough to feed the stack — the application is not the subject of the platform; it's the workload being observed."

  LAYER 2 — TELEMETRY COLLECTORS (blue cluster)
    - node-exporter (DaemonSet on k3s, host metrics)
    - OTel Collector (logs via filelog/containers receiver + traces via OTLP, exports to Loki/Jaeger)

  LAYER 3 — STORAGE / BACKENDS (cyan cluster)
    - Prometheus (metrics TSDB)
    - Loki (log aggregation)
    - Jaeger (distributed traces)
    - Drain3 state (log-template fingerprints — embedded in triage service)

  LAYER 4 — SURFACING (orange cluster)
    - Grafana (unified dashboards + alerting rules — single binary)
    - Alert rules (HighCpuUsage, HighKongP95Latency, OOMKill, LokiIngestionRateLow, etc.)
    Visual emphasis: the alert rules are the BRIDGE to Phase 2. Show them as a slightly highlighted sub-box.

  LAYER 5 (bottom) — OUTPUT EDGE (purple — pointing to Phase 2)
    - Single labelled arrow exiting downward: "→ Webhook to Phase 2 AI triage"
    - This is the "handoff" — show it visually as a clear outbound edge, like the diagram is a funnel pouring into the next page

EDGES (data flow):
  - node-exporter → Prometheus (scrape)
  - OTel Collector → Loki (logs via native loki exporter) AND → Jaeger (traces)
  - Application + Kong → OTel Collector (instrumented; container logs picked up by filelog/containers receiver from /var/lib/docker/containers/**/*-json.log)
  - Prometheus + Loki → Grafana (datasources)
  - Grafana alerting rules → Webhook (the outbound edge)

VISUAL EMPHASIS for the jury:
  - The observability stack is mature + standard (Prometheus/Loki/Jaeger/Grafana) — the jury should recognise these tools immediately
  - The application is just instrumented enough to feed the stack — it's the workload, not the subject
  - The alert rules at the bottom of Grafana are the bridge to Phase 2 — visually they should look like an "exit point"

A4 PORTRAIT FIT: 210 × 297 mm with ~12 mm safety margin. The diagram should fill the page without feeling cramped. Five horizontal layers means each layer gets roughly 50 mm of vertical space — plenty of room for clear component boxes and short annotations.

SAFETY OVERLAY ANNOTATIONS (callouts, top-right or floating beside the relevant layer):

SECTION 5 — WHAT TO HIGHLIGHT (jury-facing narrative beats)
================================================================

The diagrams must communicate these four narrative beats visually — without needing the report's prose to explain them:

  1. "MCP boundary is the safety story." The red dashed outline around the MCP cluster in Diagram 2 should be the most visually prominent element on the page. Every viewer must understand at first glance: this is a controlled boundary, not a casual abstraction.

  2. "AI layer sits ON TOP of existing observability — not replacing it." The cross-phase dashed back-arrow from MCPs to Phase 1 storage is the visual proof of integration. The two diagrams stack vertically in the report; the back-arrow makes them feel like one system.

  3. "Pipeline stages compress operator cognitive load (one row, one verdict)." The five-stage pipeline in Diagram 2 with early-exit terminals shows the gating logic — most alerts get absorbed before they ever reach the LLM. This is the operator-cognitive-load story.

  4. "Real numbers anchor it." Where appropriate, embed real production numbers as small annotations (not full labels):
     - "median 38 s end-to-end" (next to the LLM)
     - "285/285 tests" (corner annotation, Diagram 2)
     - "26 unique fingerprints from 288 raw rows" (next to Dedup stage)
     - "14 archetypes pre-injected" (next to Context Gather)
